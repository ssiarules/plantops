Three backend structures, one AI layer

PlantOps Agent does not replace the manager, the nursery, or the worker. It sits in the team chat, in Switch, and keeps the handoff between them in one place, with an approval before anything changes, and a logged outcome after.

Sample data only. No real building data. This diagram is a brainstorm and demo design, not a new build go.

Scenario A: Property management company, many buildings

Who does what, today

Building owner→Portfolio manager, central office→Building manager, per building→In-house crew, or outside vendor
  • Central office holds the portfolio view, budgets, contracts, and approvals. Building managers hold the site view, access, and daily problems.
  • Work starts as a call, text, email, or tenant note. It becomes a work order, then a vendor dispatch, then an invoice. Routine work under a set cap is often handled without owner interruption, larger work needs owner approval.
  • One portfolio relationship can put a crew in many locations, so the same crew, vendor, and budget rules repeat across buildings, with different site conditions.
Where it breaks: history lives in email and spreadsheets, not per plant. A manager in Building 2 cannot see what worked in Building 1. Vendor proof arrives as a text, not a logged outcome. Approval waits for a reply in a different thread.

Scenario B: Nursery as the service provider

Who does what, today

Nursery owner and sales→Account manager, owns a client portfolio→Crew lead, morning meeting and inspection→Field crew, shared across clients→Client buildings
  • Sales sells plants and a service plan. The account manager is the single trusted contact, owns scheduling, contracts, budgets, and client updates.
  • Crews are shared, not one crew per client. The crew lead runs the morning meeting, directs work on site, and inspects completed work.
  • Inside the nursery, a second line runs in parallel: grower and inventory, propagation to dispatch, plant quality, and replacement stock. Service and sales meet at replacement time.
Where it breaks: condition notes stay with one crew member. A route change loses the reason for the visit. A replacement is sold without a condition record to justify it. The client sees an invoice, not the photo and the check that led to it.

Scenario C: Owner managed, or owner hires help directly

Three paths from the same owner

Building owner→Path 1: In-house caretaker
Building owner→Path 2: Nursery service contract
Building owner→Path 3: Management company, then Scenario A
  • In commercial buildings, the lease decides the zone: landlord or manager usually covers base building and common areas, tenant covers its own premises, and tenant serving systems vary. Plants in common areas sit with the owner or manager, plants inside a suite sit with that tenant, unless the contract says otherwise.
  • Owner approval is direct and fast, but it is also a single point of failure. If the caretaker leaves, the history leaves with them.
  • Budget, replacement, and treatment supplies often need a separate owner approval, even when daily watering does not.
Where it breaks: no backup, no shared record, no proof. A new person starts from zero, and the owner cannot tell if a plant was watered, checked, or replaced, or why.

PlantOps Agent in between, in Switch

One room per building, not one room for everything. A portfolio summary sits above the building rooms. A nursery workspace sits above its client buildings. Permissions follow the room: manager sees their buildings, crew sees only assigned buildings, nursery manager sees all clients, client manager sees only their own buildings.

Signal: photo, dry soil, schedule, weather, irrigation→Agent triage: register, weather, irrigation, care log→Proposed work order
Manager Approve or Decline, in chat→Worker checklist, assigned crew→Photo plus condition note
Suggestions, how to, tool needed→Work done, photo proof→Manager verifies→Logged outcome, closed

Then, Phase 5, draft only: the agent drafts an email with the work order document, memo, and outcome log attached, posts it in the room for manager approval, and only then would it send, to sample recipients in the demo. No irrigation change, vendor dispatch, or payment happens before manager approval. Escalation triggers go to the manager in the room. Suggestions are not a certain diagnosis from one photo.

Who benefits, in each scenario

PartyWhat changes with PlantOps in the room
OwnerPortfolio health in one view, not one building at a time. Approval only for work above the cap, with a record for every job.
Property managerOne tap Approve or Decline in chat, instead of chasing email. Same work order format across buildings, so staff can cover for each other.
Nursery managerAll clients, urgent conditions first, crew assigned by building. Replacements are justified by condition and photo, not by a sales call alone.
Crew and workerA checklist for that plant, that area, that zone, plus how to and tool needed, instead of a vague task. Photo proof protects their work.
Client managerProof in the room: photo, condition note, work done, verified, closed. Not just an invoice at month end.

How this maps to the demo, without breaking the core

  • Core, already built: one sample building, one work order, one approval, one logged outcome, in a real Switch room. That stays the safe fallback.
  • Second scene, talk track: show the same sample building from the nursery side, client, building, planter, and show the portfolio summary with two more sample buildings. No new code needed to talk through it.
  • Phase 3, if approved: sample floor plan and plant card, work order document on Approve, tag sample crew first, vendor last and talk track only.
  • Phase 4, in review: worker checklist, photo step, suggestions, how to, tool, escalation, work done with photo proof, manager verifies, closed.
  • Phase 5, confirmed draft only: email draft with attachment as second documentation, memo, work order document, outcome log, synced to manager, nursery, and worker, after manager approves the draft in the room.
